# Revised Introduction and Contributions

## Copy-ready Introduction

Video world models are increasingly used as surrogates for robot–environment interaction: to evaluate policies, generate training trajectories, and support planning. In these applications, generated rollouts inform decisions that would otherwise require interaction with a real or simulated environment. Their usefulness therefore depends on more than the visual plausibility of individual frames. The robot must retain its structure as it moves, and different views must describe a consistent scene. A rollout that violates these requirements can look convincing while providing misleading evidence about the behavior it depicts.

Existing evaluations provide several complementary but incomplete signals. Reference-based image metrics, such as PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, measure visual similarity without explicitly testing structural or physical constraints. Temporal smoothness scores can also favor static predictions, even when the task requires motion. Physics-oriented benchmarks, including VideoPhy and PhyGenBench, assess physical plausibility through vision-language model judgments, whose reliability must itself be validated. Grounded benchmarks move closer to explicit measurement: EWMBench evaluates embodied motion and scene consistency, while RoboPhys-3D combines reconstruction-based measurements with state- and task-level evaluation. These approaches motivate a complementary evaluation target: detecting structural inconsistency within a generated robot body and geometric or temporal disagreement between different generated views of the same episode.

Robotic generation provides useful structure for defining these checks. The embodiment, camera layout, and task context are often known before generation, allowing the evaluator to specify which entities and relationships should remain consistent. We exploit this setting to examine whether the robot preserves its structure within each view and whether multiple views remain mutually compatible over time. These checks produce explicit evidence of inconsistency, while remaining subject to visibility and errors in the underlying perception tools. They test observable requirements of a coherent rollout rather than certify that every depicted interaction obeys all physical laws.

We introduce [BenchName], a benchmark for the structural and spatiotemporal fidelity of robotic video world models. It covers single-arm, bimanual, and humanoid or mobile dual-arm settings, with single- and multi-view observations and atomic and sequential task subsets. We evaluate Ctrl-World, DreamDojo, and DreamGen under their supported configurations, distinguishing action-conditioned generation from image- and text-conditioned generation. The core diagnostics assess embodiment integrity through temporal consistency of body structure and motion, and cross-view coherence through geometric compatibility and temporal stability across views. We complement these checks with motion diagnostics and, for accelerated generation, fidelity to the corresponding dense rollout. The core consistency scores use explicit measurements rather than a generative vision-language judge, with localized outputs that support inspection of when and where inconsistencies occur.

Beyond evaluating dense generation, these diagnostics provide a way to examine the fidelity cost of inference acceleration. Caching, sparse attention, and token reduction reduce computation, but their effects need not be uniform across the properties of a generated rollout. We use [BenchName] to compare dense and accelerated outputs within matched evaluation settings, asking whether faster generation preserves body structure, motion, and agreement across views. We distinguish absolute consistency violations from deviations relative to the dense output: agreement with a dense reference measures preservation of that reference, not physical correctness by itself. This turns acceleration evaluation into a study of the speed–fidelity trade-off for individual properties, rather than speed and a single aggregate quality score.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we organize a benchmark for robotic video generation with explicit model coverage, conditioning modalities, and evaluation settings. Second, we provide property-specific diagnostics of embodiment integrity and cross-view coherence, complemented by motion and dense-reference measurements. Third, we use these diagnostics to study inference acceleration across supported model–method combinations, separating consistency violations from changes relative to dense generation.

## Expanded Contributions

Use this list instead of the final Introduction paragraph when the paper format calls for a separate contribution list.

1. **A structured benchmark for robotic video world models.** We introduce [BenchName], a benchmark for observable structural and spatiotemporal fidelity in generated robotic rollouts. It covers three embodiment categories, single- and multi-view observations, and atomic and sequential task subsets. We document model-specific coverage, conditioning modalities, and adaptation protocols, distinguishing action-conditioned prediction from image- and text-conditioned generation.

2. **Property-specific diagnostics of embodiment and cross-view consistency.** We assess whether robot structure remains consistent within each view and whether generated views of the same episode remain geometrically and temporally compatible. These checks provide localized evidence of inconsistency without a generative vision-language judge. We distinguish the core structural diagnostics from supporting motion measurements and fidelity to a dense reference, so that different sources of failure remain separately inspectable.

3. **An analysis of inference acceleration and fidelity.** We evaluate nine accelerators across their supported model configurations: PISA, Radial, SVG1, and SVG2 for sparse attention; SiTo and iTM for token reduction; and DiCache, WorldCache, and FasterCache for temporal reuse. Dense and accelerated rollouts are compared within matched settings to characterize changes in individual properties. The analysis separates absolute consistency violations from deviation relative to dense generation, providing a framework for assessing the speed–fidelity trade-off without proposing a new accelerator.

## Editorial Notes — Not for Copying into the Paper

- **Scope:** bản này dùng “robotic video world models” vì repo có cả action-conditioned và image/text-conditioned generation. Không gọi mọi model là action-conditioned.
- **Embodiment:** “humanoid or mobile dual-arm” phản ánh khác biệt GR1 single-view và Airbot MMK2 multi-view. Nếu giữ “three embodiment categories”, dataset section phải liệt kê robot cụ thể.
- **Task subsets:** dùng atomic/sequential thay Markovian/non-Markovian rollout regimes. Nhãn task hiện có chưa chứng minh Markov property hoặc can thiệp history của generator.
- **Views:** không dùng “independently generated” vì repo có joint strip/grid generation. Cross-view checking vẫn có ý nghĩa với jointly generated outputs.
- **Counts:** chưa đưa số tasks/episodes/videos vào bản copy vì draft có số không thống nhất và canonical output chưa hoàn thành ở thời điểm review. Chỉ thêm sau khi freeze manifest và phân biệt source episodes, camera videos, generated rollouts, segments, valid scored outputs.
- **Citations:** thêm citation keys tương ứng cho policy evaluation, synthetic data, planning, các benchmark trong P2 và accelerator list. Đây là bản viết lại, chưa phải bản bibliography hoàn chỉnh. Related Work cần đối chiếu thêm MiraBench, RoboWM-Bench và Physics-IQ; không dùng claim “first” hoặc “none of the existing benchmarks”.
- **Validation:** bản này không claim human agreement, AUC, false-positive rate hay family-specific degradation findings. Khi có held-out validation, thêm một câu kết quả vào P4; khi matched acceleration sweep hoàn thành, thêm một finding cụ thể vào P5.
- **Completion:** các câu “we provide” và “we evaluate” là cách viết paper cho phạm vi dự kiến. Trước submission, cần xác minh source evaluator tái lập được, metric coverage và matched configs cho mọi kết quả được report. Nếu chỉ hoàn thành subset, ghi rõ subset.
- **Judge scope:** “without a generative vision-language judge” không đồng nghĩa không dùng learned perception. Method section phải công khai detector, tracker, depth/flow/interpolation backends và mọi text-based gate.
- **Tone:** giới hạn về observability được giữ một câu ở P3 để claim chính xác. Có thể chuyển phần giải thích dài hơn sang Methods/Limitations khi paper đã định nghĩa measurement assumptions rõ.

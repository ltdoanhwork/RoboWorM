## C(i) — Inference acceleration: speed vs. fidelity trade-off

**Figure (main text):** `assets/paper_figures/speedup_star_chart.pdf`

**Caption draft:**

> **Figure X. Speed–fidelity trade-off of inference acceleration methods.**
> Each radial axis is normalized so that the dense baseline (dashed black,
> value 1.0 on every axis) forms a perfect reference square at radius 0.5;
> a method's polygon extends beyond dense on an axis when it *improves*
> that metric (faster, or higher PSNR/SSIM, or lower LPIPS than dense) and
> shrinks inward when it degrades it. One panel per model family
> (Ctrl-World, DreamGen, DreamDojo); a method appears in a panel only if it
> was benchmarked on that family with a measured or documented speedup — we
> do not plot fabricated points for untested combinations. Values are means
> over all embodiment/view/split cells with a recorded speedup for that
> (method, family) pair (see `metrics/reports/speedup_summary_full.csv` for
> the underlying cells and `speedup_summary_appendix.tex` for the full
> per-cell table, Appendix §Y).

**Main-text paragraph draft:**

> We evaluate nine acceleration methods spanning three families — whole-step
> caching (DiCache, WorldCache, FasterCache), spatial-token pruning (SiTo,
> ITM), and sparse/structured attention (PISA, Radial, SVG1, SVG2) — against
> the dense baseline across all three model families (Ctrl-World, DreamGen,
> DreamDojo) and applicable embodiments/views. Figure X summarizes the
> speed–fidelity trade-off as a per-family radar chart; Table Y (Appendix)
> reports every individual (method, embodiment, view, split) cell.
>
> **Caching methods give the largest, safest speedups.** Averaged across all
> benchmarked cells, DiCache reaches **1.50×** speedup at **98.0%** mean
> quality retention (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS-normalized vs. dense), and WorldCache
> **1.47×** at **97.0%** retention — both dominate every other method on the
> speed axis while staying visually indistinguishable from dense on the
> quality axes. FasterCache is the weakest cache backend (**1.16×**,
> 100.4% retention over the cells where it is compatible): on Ctrl-World's
> custom SVD U-Net it is a documented no-op (the model exposes no
> `attn_processors` and runs at `guidance_scale=1`, so FasterCache's
> uncond-skip path never triggers), a negative result we keep as a valid
> benchmark point rather than discard.
>
> **Sparse/structured attention gives modest, family-dependent speedups.**
> PISA is the most consistent winner in this family (**1.23×**, 90.5%
> retention), followed by Radial (**1.20×**, 94.4% retention — the best
> quality retention of any non-caching method) and SVG1 (**1.15×**, 89.9%).
> SVG2 is net-negative overall (**0.97×**): its block-sparse `flashinfer`
> kernel is repeatedly slower than dense flash-attention at this model
> family's `(S≈4800, 16 heads, D=128)` shape (measured directly via
> `scripts/_diag_dreamdojo_svg.py`), so no amount of re-tuning closes the
> gap — a kernel limitation, not a tuning failure. On the DreamDojo
> singleview family specifically, self-attention is only ~15–18% of total
> generation time (profiled with `DD_ATTN_PROFILE=1`), which caps *any*
> sparse-attention method at ≈1.1–1.2× regardless of kernel quality —
> caching remains the only path to large speedups there.
>
> **Token pruning is the weakest and most fragile family.** SiTo
> (**1.13×**, 84.6% retention) and ITM (**1.15×**, 75.8% retention) only
> exceed ~1.1× using the spatio-temporal "prune-once-hold" variant, and both
> have a hard quality cliff at `keep_ratio ≤ 0.5–0.7` depending on the
> target. Profiling shows why: spatial attention is only ~7% of Ctrl-World's
> per-step wall time (conv/ResNet/temporal blocks dominate at ~74%), so
> per-token pruning of spatial attention alone structurally cannot deliver
> large, safe speedups on this architecture family.
>
> Overall ranking by mean speedup at ≥90% quality retention: **DiCache >
> WorldCache > PISA > Radial > SVG1 ≈ ITM ≈ SiTo > FasterCache > SVG2.**
> Table Y in the appendix reports every individual cell (embodiment × view ×
> split) so family- or embodiment-specific deviations from this global
> ranking (e.g. ITM beating SiTo on Ctrl-World humanoid multiview, PISA's
> universal win on DreamGen) remain visible rather than averaged away.

**Appendix table:** `metrics/reports/speedup_summary_appendix.tex`
(auto-generated `longtable`, one row per benchmarked cell). Intro sentence
for the appendix:

> Table Y reports per-cell PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS and speedup for every
> (model family, embodiment, view, method) combination we benchmarked.
> Speedup values marked † are transcribed from the documented tuning runs
> in this repo's method-tuning notes (`.cursor/rules/*-insights.mdc`) rather
> than the full sampling-dataset sweep, because per-episode wall-clock time
> is only persisted in `all_summary.json` for a subset of cells (chiefly
> Ctrl-World multiview); all † numbers come from a run of the *default*
> configuration of that script on ≥1 val episode of the same model/embodiment,
> and are the same numbers used to set that script's shipped defaults.
> Blank speedup cells were benchmarked for quality but have no recorded
> timing under either provenance and are reported as not-yet-measured
> rather than omitted or estimated.

## B — Effect of embodiment fine-tuning (checkpoint ablation)

**Figure (main text):** `assets/paper_figures/checkpoint_finetune_comparison.pdf`

**Caption draft:**

> **Figure Z. Fine-tuning on embodiment-specific data substantially improves
> generation fidelity over the released base checkpoint.** Grouped bars
> compare the released, non-finetuned checkpoint ("Base") against the
> embodiment-finetuned checkpoint used everywhere else in this paper as the
> `dense` baseline ("Finetuned"), evaluated on the *same* held-out episodes
> with the *same* inference pipeline (only the checkpoint weights differ).
> PSNR/SSIM: higher is better; LPIPS: lower is better.

**Main-text paragraph draft:**

> To isolate the contribution of embodiment-specific fine-tuning (as opposed
> to model architecture or acceleration method), we re-run the exact dense
> inference pipeline with the released, non-finetuned checkpoint in place of
> the finetuned one, holding the episode set, sampler settings, and scoring
> code fixed. We test this on the two model families / embodiments where a
> genuine base checkpoint exists and is architecture-compatible with the
> finetuned config: Ctrl-World (base = the released `checkpoint-10000.pt`
> DROID pretrain) and DreamGen (base =
> `Cosmos-Predict2-2B-Video2World/model-480p-16fps.pt`), each on bimanual and
> humanoid multiview.
>
> Fine-tuning improves every metric, on every cell, by a wide and consistent
> margin (Fig. Z; Table W): Ctrl-World bimanual multiview PSNR rises from
> **14.72 → 20.66 dB (+5.94 dB)**, SSIM 0.578 → 0.772, LPIPS 0.523 → 0.299;
> Ctrl-World humanoid multiview PSNR rises **14.25 → 21.02 dB (+6.76 dB)**,
> SSIM 0.660 → 0.838, LPIPS 0.440 → 0.206. DreamGen shows the same direction
> at a lower absolute level (multiview DiT models start from a much lower
> dense ceiling on this benchmark, ~12–13 dB, regardless of checkpoint):
> bimanual PSNR **9.26 → 13.35 dB (+4.09 dB)**, humanoid PSNR
> **8.77 → 12.90 dB (+4.14 dB)**, with matching SSIM/LPIPS gains. The base
> checkpoint is not merely worse — its predictions visibly lose scene/actor
> structure specific to each embodiment's camera rig and action space, which
> the finetuned checkpoint recovers. This confirms that the fidelity levels
> reported for `dense` throughout this benchmark reflect the value added by
> embodiment fine-tuning, not just architecture or training-data scale, and
> that any acceleration method's quality-retention numbers (Section C(i))
> are being measured against a meaningfully-tuned, non-trivial baseline.
>
> **Scope and a known blocker (for the appendix / limitations paragraph):**
> we exclude single_arm multiview from this ablation for both families
> because their `dense` baselines already run the un-finetuned base
> checkpoint (Ctrl-World's `checkpoint-10000.pt`, DreamGen's
> `GR00T-Dreams-DROID`) — there is no finetuned counterpart to compare
> against. We exclude DreamDojo (bimanual/single_arm singleview) because,
> in the current environment, `ActionConditionedSetupArguments` eagerly
> resolves an unrelated checkpoint reference from the gated HuggingFace repo
> `nvidia/Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B` at construction time regardless of
> `--checkpoints-dir`; we verified this 401s for the *existing finetuned*
> checkpoint path too, i.e. it is a pre-existing account-level access
> restriction in this environment, not an artifact of selecting the base
> checkpoint. We exclude DreamDojo humanoid because only one checkpoint
> (`2B_GR1_post-train`) was ever produced for that embodiment — there is no
> separate base/finetuned pair. We exclude DreamGen singleview (humanoid,
> single_arm) because that pipeline only ever writes `.mp4`/`.txt` outputs
> (no `metrics.json`), so neither checkpoint has a fidelity score to compare
> — extending this ablation there would require adding a scorer, which is
> out of scope here.

**Table (appendix or inline):** `metrics/reports/checkpoint_finetune_comparison.csv`
— 4 rows (Ctrl-World × {bimanual, humanoid}, DreamGen × {bimanual, humanoid}),
columns: base/finetuned PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS + deltas, checkpoint paths, n_episodes.

## Known limitations to state explicitly in the paper (do not silently drop)

1. **Mixed timing provenance in C(i).** Most DreamGen/DreamDojo cells have no
   persisted per-episode wall-clock time in `sampling_dataset/`; their
   speedup numbers come from this repo's own documented tuning runs (†
   footnote in the appendix table), not a fresh full-sweep timing pass. Only
   Ctrl-World multiview (bimanual/single_arm/humanoid) has persisted
   `mean_wall_time_s` for a subset of methods.
2. **69 of 127 benchmarked cells have quality but no speedup number at all**
   (mostly Ctrl-World bimanual/single_arm with PISA/Radial/SVG1/SVG2/SiTo/ITM,
   where no tuning-insight rule documents a timing and no
   `mean_wall_time_s` was persisted). These are reported as blank/omitted in
   the appendix, not estimated.
3. **B is a 4-cell, small-N ablation** (1–2 episodes per cell), not a
   full-dataset sweep — appropriate for demonstrating the *direction and
   rough magnitude* of the fine-tuning effect, not a tight confidence
   interval. Extending it to more episodes is mechanical (rerun
   `scripts/_run_checkpoint_ablation_base.sh` with a larger `NUM_EPISODES` /
   `MAX_EPISODES`), and to DreamDojo once the gated HF-repo access is
   resolved for this account.

# Robot Video Quality Assessment Guide

## Task

You will watch robot videos generated by a model and rate their hallucination level — artifacts that a video of a real robot would never contain. Robot quality covers whether the robot is deformed, blurred, color-bleeding, moving strangely, or moving in a way that violates ordinary physics.

Each video is split into short segments. You rate every segment on the scale:

**Scale:** Good – Medium – Bad

where Good means little to no deformation — the segment looks like a clean video.

Note: rate each segment independently. A segment may be corrupted while the robot recovers and looks clean again in later segments — those later segments should still be rated Good; do not let an earlier segment influence a later one.

## What to focus on

Focus on the robot itself:

- Shape: is the robot deformed, stretched, broken apart, missing its arm/gripper, blurred, or dissolving?
- Color / surface: any color bleeding, flickering, or abnormal color changes?
- Motion: is it smooth and physically plausible like a real robot — or jittery, teleporting between positions, or bending joints in impossible ways?
- Temporal consistency: is the robot in a later frame still the same robot as in earlier frames (no missing details, no extra parts appearing)?
- Camera stability: ideally the camera is fully static; noticeable shaking or abnormal rotation/translation counts as an error.

## Rule for abnormal events

Abnormal events happening to the robot itself count as errors, including but not limited to: the robot growing an extra arm or part; losing its arm/gripper; suddenly moving or jumping to another position; strange or non-physical behavior; deforming, disappearing / appearing suddenly. Only the robot is considered — not other objects in the frame.

## What not to consider

- The robot's interaction with the environment (whether a grasp succeeds, whether the task is completed).
- Video compression quality or resolution.

## Scoring criteria (per segment)

| Level | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Good** | The robot looks like a real video: intact shape, stable colors, smooth and physically plausible motion. Any deformation is tiny and momentary. Camera nearly static. |
| **Medium** | Visible errors (mild deformation, local blur, slight jitter, brief color bleeding), but the robot is still clearly recognizable and its action can still be followed. Camera may stutter or shift/rotate slightly. |
| **Bad** | Clear, severe errors lasting through the segment: heavy deformation or dissolving, teleporting, non-physical motion; the action is hard or impossible to follow. Camera may rotate/shift with large amplitude. |
